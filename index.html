AI FAQ for Teachers

Your practical guide to using AI tools in education

Getting Started

Start with something familiar and low-stakes. Try having AI help you write a parent email, or create sentence stems. Compare the AI output to what you would have created yourself - this helps you understand how the AI thinks and produces content.

You have access to NSW EduChat, start there as it's designed for education. Otherwise, ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are way more user-friendly and versatile for professionals. Each of these has a version of a resource panel that you can iterate and refine.

Just use common sense when entering information. A good rule of thumb, if you wouldn't share it on a Facebook group, don't put it into an AI tool.

Effective Usage

Be specific and provide context. Instead of "Create a lesson plan," try "Create a 45-minute Year 3 science lesson about plant life cycles, including a hands-on activity for 25 students with limited materials." The more details you provide, the better the output.

Better yet, brainstorm strategies and goals with AI first. Create a grounding context document with curriculum points and assessment goals to copy and paste in a new conversation.

  • Ask AI to surface differentiation strategies and approaches to make learning more accessible.
  • Instruct it to use Universal Design For Learning principles when creating resources.

AI is good at creating rubrics, generating diverse question types, and helping phrase constructive feedback. Feed it the overall assessment goal: "Using this assessment task, create a 4-level rubric for assessing Year 6 persuasive writing with clear criteria and student-friendly language."

For feedback, you'll bring the teacher lens, AI can help with phrasing it quickly. Your expertise is key.

Administrative Tasks

AI is excellent for drafting routine communications. Give it the key points and ask for appropriate tone. For example: "Write a polite email to parents explaining that their child needs to complete missing homework. Keep it supportive and solution-focused."

AI can quickly generate content, but always review for accuracy and appropriateness. Try: "Create 10 word problems for Year 5 multiplication using real-world scenarios that would interest 10-11 year olds."

AI can help generate comment bank phrases and suggest ways to phrase observations constructively. But remember - the insights about individual students obviously come from you.

Quality Control

Always fact-check important information, especially in subjects like science, history, or current events. Use AI as a starting point, not the final word. Cross-reference with reliable sources. If you haven’t uploaded or pasted the context yourself, assume the content has come from the AI memory and may have inconsistencies. Even content from ‘Search’ hallucinates.

Avoid AI for:

  • Final grades or high-stakes assessments without review
  • Sensitive communications with parents about serious issues
  • Content about controversial topics without fact-checking
  • Anything that replaces your professional judgment about individual students

Tips and Tricks

  • Be specific about format: "Create a table with three columns" or "Write this as bullet points"
  • Specify audience: "Write this for parents"
  • Ask for examples: "Include three specific examples"
  • Request multiple options: "Give me three different approaches"
  • Load it with context: Give it documents, paste text, tell it what to use. Try to convert to markdown if possible to give structure and minimise context overload.

Markdown is a simple text format that provides structure for AI.

Hold 'shift' and 'return' to get a new line without sending the message.

Iterative Prompting: It won't be right on the first try. Iterate...

  • Start with a basic request
  • Ask for modifications: "Make this more engaging for reluctant readers"
  • Request additions: "Add an extension activity for fast finishers"
  • Refine tone: "Make the language more formal/casual/encouraging"
